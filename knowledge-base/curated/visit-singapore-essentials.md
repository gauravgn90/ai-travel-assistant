# Singapore Travel Essentials

Practical visitor information for Singapore: climate, money, connectivity, language, and the
everyday conventions that catch first-time visitors out.

## Climate and when to go

Singapore sits one degree north of the equator and has no seasons in the temperate sense. Daytime
highs sit between 30 and 33 °C year round, overnight lows between 24 and 26 °C, and relative
humidity commonly exceeds 80%. Because of the humidity, the apparent temperature is usually several
degrees above the measured one.

Rain falls in every month. The wettest stretch is the Northeast Monsoon from November to January,
when prolonged afternoon and evening downpours are common. February to April is drier and hotter.
The Southwest Monsoon from June to September brings shorter, sharper showers, often in the late
afternoon, plus the occasional haze episode caused by regional forest fires.

The practical consequence for planning is that rain is a scheduling problem rather than a
trip-ruining one. Showers are usually intense and brief. A day plan that pairs each outdoor block
with a nearby covered option loses very little to weather.

## Money

The currency is the Singapore dollar (SGD, written S$), divided into 100 cents. Notes circulate in
S$2, S$5, S$10, S$50, S$100 and S$1000; coins in 5, 10, 20 and 50 cents and S$1.

Card acceptance is close to universal, including at most hawker stalls, which typically take
PayNow QR or contactless cards. Carry a small amount of cash anyway: a handful of older stalls,
wet-market vendors and some Pulau Ubin operators remain cash-only.

Tipping is not customary. Restaurants that add service typically show "++" on the menu, meaning a
10% service charge plus 9% GST are added at the till. Taxis are paid to the meter with no tip
expected.

Tourists spending at least S$100 at participating retailers can claim a GST refund at Changi
Airport through the eTRS system, provided the goods leave with them.

## Getting around

The MRT is the default way to move around the city and reaches nearly every visitor destination.
Trains run roughly 05:30 to midnight. Fares are distance-based and most journeys cost between S$1
and S$2.50.

Payment options, cheapest first for most visitors:

- A foreign contactless Visa, Mastercard or Amex card, or the same card in Apple Pay or Google Pay.
  Tap in and out. A small daily handling fee applies to foreign-issued cards.
- An EZ-Link or NETS prepaid card, bought at any MRT station or 7-Eleven and topped up with cash.
- The Singapore Tourist Pass, which buys unlimited travel for one to three days. It only pays for
  itself on heavy travel days, because ordinary fares are so low.

Cash is not accepted on the MRT. Buses take cash but give no change. Always tap out on buses, or
the maximum fare is charged. Eating and drinking, including water, is prohibited on trains and in
stations.

Ride-hailing through Grab and Gojek is widely used and reasonably priced. Street taxis are metered
and honest; surcharges apply for peak hours, late nights, and airport pickups.

## Language and communication

There are four official languages: English, Mandarin, Malay and Tamil. English is the language of
government, business, education and signage, so no language preparation is needed. Colloquial
Singlish mixes in Malay and Hokkien vocabulary and clipped sentence structure, but speakers switch
to standard English readily.

Free public Wi-Fi is available through Wireless@SG at libraries, malls, MRT stations and many
cafés. Prepaid tourist SIM and eSIM packages are sold at Changi Airport arrival halls and
convenience stores.

## Electricity and connectivity

Mains power is 230 V at 50 Hz. Sockets take the UK-style Type G three-pin plug, so visitors from
most of Europe, North America and Asia need an adapter.

## Practical conventions

Singapore is among the safest cities in the world for visitors, including for solo travellers and
at night, but enforcement of minor rules is genuinely strict and applies to tourists.

- Littering, jaywalking and smoking outside designated areas carry fines.
- Chewing gum cannot be imported or sold, though possessing gum for personal use is not an offence.
- Drug offences carry severe penalties, including capital punishment for trafficking.
- Tap water is treated to WHO standards and safe to drink.
- Dress is casual and lightweight. Temples and mosques expect covered shoulders and knees; several
  lend robes at the entrance.
- Remove shoes before entering mosques, Hindu temples and private homes.
- Air conditioning indoors is aggressive. A light layer is worth carrying even on the hottest day.

## Accessibility

Singapore is one of Asia's most step-free cities. All MRT stations have lifts and tactile guidance,
public buses are wheelchair-accessible, and major attractions, malls and hawker centres have ramp
or lift access. Covered walkways link many MRT stations to nearby malls and housing blocks, which
matters both for rain and for heat.

## Emergencies and health

Police 999, ambulance and fire 995, non-emergency ambulance 1777. No vaccinations are required for
entry from most countries. Dengue is present year round, so repellent is sensible for extended time
in parks and nature reserves. Public hospitals and private clinics are of a high standard; travel
insurance is recommended because treatment is not free for visitors.
